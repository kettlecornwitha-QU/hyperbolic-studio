A SHORT FIELD GUIDE

From a disk to a surface

The disk is the entire hyperbolic plane. Geodesics are circular arcs perpendicular to its boundary. The apparent shrinking comes from the metric ds = 2|dz|/(1−|z|²).

  1. Choose a tiling. Primary polygons, their perpendicular dual, and center-to-vertex edges form a checkerboard of small geodesic triangles. Center-to-vertex edges have a sixth color, are thinner and fainter by default, and have a separate width control.
  2. Cmd-click as many segments as you need to select two continuous straight chains. Straightness is measured along hyperbolic geodesics, which may look curved in the disk. Chains may mix primary, dual, and center-to-vertex segments, but their segment types and lengths must match in order (possibly reversed). Selected segments are highlighted. Ctrl-click also works.
  3. Click Identify selected edges, then click one highlighted outer endpoint on each edge to specify which endpoints are identified. Endpoints do not need names. Choose the outer ends of each chain, rather than any interior dot.
  4. After setting the orientation, enter one shared name and save. The pair appears once in Identified Edges, with one chevron style for the whole identification. Hover its row to highlight both edges. Click its name to rename it, or × to remove it. Escape or Cancel abandons an unfinished identification.
  5. Click any dot to give it a unique navigation name. It appears in Vertices. Hover to highlight it; click its name to edit or use ⌾ to center the disk on it.
  6. In Region Construction mode, click a dual vertex (primary center) to select its primary polygon, or a primary vertex to select its dual polygon. A crossing dot selects its four triangles; an edge segment selects its two triangles; clicking inside a triangle selects just that triangle. If a whole clicked group is already selected, the click removes it; otherwise it adds the missing triangles. The region can mix all these selections freely. Read Topology for closure, vertex-link checks, orientability, Euler characteristic, genus or crosscaps, area, and cone angles.

Try the genus 2 example. The regular {8,8} octagon has interior angles π/4. Opposite sides, paired with reversed boundary directions, produce a smooth genus 2 surface. Its area is 4π at K = −1.

Try the Macbeath example. The Fricke–Macbeath surface is built from 72 regular {7,3} heptagons using the PSL(2,8) quotient of the (2,3,7) triangle group. Its 504 orientation-preserving symmetries attain the Hurwitz bound. The displayed connected region has 97 named boundary-edge pairs. Expected results: genus 7, χ = −12, area 24π, and all vertex angles 2π. Pan to examine the boundary and hover an Identified Edges row to see both sides of a gluing. The triangle subdivision gives V = 492, E = 1512, F = 1008.

A topological surface can have cone points: the angle sum around a glued vertex must be 2π for the displayed metric to be smooth. Red or amber diagnostics tell you what remains to check.

Mixed tilings: truncated triheptagonal means squares, hexagons, and 14-gons (4.6.14). Its dual polygons are not regular. The crossings bisect primary edges; for a mixed tiling they need not bisect dual edges.

Navigation limits: this is a finite numerical patch of an infinite tiling. Increase patch size if you reach its edge; Reset view returns to its center. Picking is enabled only where objects remain visually distinct. Widths and dots use hyperbolic units; hovering and selection add a screen-space halo.

Projects auto-save locally in this browser; Save updates the chosen file in browsers supporting direct file access. Save As chooses a new file. Other browsers download a JSON copy with the chosen name; they may add a number rather than overwrite. Cmd/Ctrl+S saves; Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+S uses Save As. The mode dropdown above the disk works in both views. Fullscreen places the Construction/Topology pane beside the disk; use Exit fullscreen or Escape to return. Switching tilings starts a separate construction, so save first if you want to keep the current one.

Useful next experiments: reverse a pairing to see orientability change; compare vertex angle sums; select several tiles and inspect how the quotient cell counts change.
